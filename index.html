№ 03 · the actual flagship
PaperTrail
Chrome Extension + Companion Web App
the logo
~ish ~
by a kid

A Chrome extension that reviews Google Docs writing history and flags large paste events — so authorship is something you can show, not something you have to swear to. The companion web app, PaperTrail Write, is a monitored writing surface for when you need a clean room: tracks time-on-task, pastes, tab-switching, and quietly-deleted paste-ins. No keystrokes, no clipboard, nothing off-page. Turnitin tells you if it was copied. PaperTrail tells you if it was theirs.

№ 04 · the whole arcade
Classroom Games
AP Lang · TOK · IB Core · AP Research

Roughly two dozen single-purpose tools for the courses I teach. AP Lang: Hexagonal Thinking, Commentary Workshop, Evidence Hunt, Thesis Statements, Rhetorical Analysis Sprint, an Unpopular Opinion Generator that exists mostly to teach semicolons. TOK: The Map is Not the Territory, Paradigm Shifts, Power & Knowledge, Earthrise (Apollo 8 as a TOK object). IB Core: a Dashboard for the three things students forget about until April. AP Research: a literature-review organiser. The whole thing is held together by a Google Sheets backend — the duct tape and bubble gum of modern coding.

№ 05
Classroom Research
AI Detection · Assessment

A small archive of data studies from real classrooms. Headline reports: 91 papers across AP Lang and IB amnesty programs (77% confession rate, <1% false positives, +63% flagging Sep 2025–Jan 2026); an internal Turnitin validation against 44 documents of known authorship; and a plain-language parent summary. Includes a supervised writing protocol for flagged work.

№ 06
agent/smith
Personal Stock Market Theses

My running collection of stock-market theses, served as a daily-tape terminal. Scans the news (yes, including Truth Social), watches the VIX, classifies the day's moves as overdone or underdone, and tells me which positions to actually pay attention to. Powered by Claude Opus 4.7. You're welcome to follow along and put real money on its read of the day. So far: up, somewhere north of $400. I would almost certainly be wealthier — and have considerably more weekends — if I'd just bought the S&P. Any gains are obviously skill. Any losses are directly attributable to Claude, not M. Smith.
