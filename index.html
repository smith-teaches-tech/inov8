Ch. I
The SaaS attempt
Two products, one philosophy, $4.99 in revenue.
№ 01 · the one I'm trying to make money with
PaperTrail Write
Monitored Writing · SaaS

A browser-based, monitored writing surface. Tracks time-on-task, paste events, tab-switching, and whether pasted text gets quietly deleted afterwards. Does not log keystrokes, clipboard contents, or anything off-page. The teacher gets a session report; the student gets a clean place to write. Lifetime revenue to date: $4.99. We are, technically, profitable on a per-bagel basis.

REV: $4.99 · YTD ↗ № 02
PaperTrail (Extension)
Chrome Extension

The Chrome companion. Reviews Google Docs writing history and surfaces large paste events — so authorship is something you can show, not something you have to swear to. Turnitin tells you if it was copied. This tells you if it was theirs.

Ch. II
AP Language
Rhetoric, syntax, argument, style — gamified, mostly responsibly.
№ 03
Hexagonal Thinking
Synthesis

Drag hexagons. Snap them to other hexagons. Justify the connection. Looks like a board game. Is, in fact, an argument scaffold.

↗ № 04
Commentary Workshop
Line of Reasoning · MCI

Teaches commentary to move down rather than sideways: Mechanism, Consequence, Implication. A walkthrough that gradually releases the student until they're writing the bridge themselves. The "just keep digging" model of paragraph development.

↗ № 05
ARG: Claims, Evidence & Commentary
Argument Building

The trinity, drilled. Students assemble argument paragraphs from parts and find out the hard way that evidence without commentary is just trivia. The unit's foundation, in playable form.

↗ № 06
High School Drama: Synthesis
Synthesis Practice

A synthesis prompt dressed up as a hallway scandal. Students weigh competing sources to take a position on something low-stakes enough to argue about, high-stakes enough to care. Synthesis, but make it personal.

↗ № 07
Unpopular Opinion Generator
Punctuation Drill

Generates a take you'd never voluntarily defend, then asks you to defend it in 300 words using semicolons, colons, and dashes. Sophisticated punctuation is easier when the topic is "pineapple belongs on pizza."

↗ № 08
Evidence Hunt
Live Collaboration

A live, classroom-wide scavenger hunt for textual evidence. Students post, vote, refine. The whiteboard, finally, scales. Surprisingly competitive. Sometimes too competitive.

↗ № 09
Crafting Thesis Statements
The Top of the Page

A guided builder for the sentence that determines everything beneath it. Models, anti-models, and a worked path from "kind of has a point" to "defensible, arguable, and worth the reader's time." Where most essays are won or quietly lost.

↗ № 10
Rhetorical Analysis Sprint
RA · SPACE Annotation

Timed rhetorical reading drills with SPACE annotation built in. Mark up the passage, submit, move on. Designed to feel less like an essay and more like a workout.

Ch. III
Theory of Knowledge
For asking "but how do we know that?" — preferably in class, not at dinner.
№ 11
The Map is Not the Territory
Representation · AOK

Two maps of the same place. Both true. Neither complete. A lesson on representation, abstraction, and the fact that every map is also a choice about what to leave out. Korzybski, but with clickable maps.

↗ № 12
Paradigm Shifts
Knowledge · History · Methods

When the consensus changes, what counted as knowledge yesterday becomes the historical curiosity of tomorrow. Kuhn, gently introduced, with examples that don't require a PhD to see the point. The lesson where geocentrism does the heavy lifting.

↗ № 13
Power and Knowledge
Politics · Perspective

Who decides what counts as knowing? Whose questions get funded, whose books get assigned, whose claims get the benefit of the doubt? Foucault, lightly. Just enough to ruin the news for them forever.

↗ № 14
Earthrise: Values, Interpretation, Objectivity
Object · The Big Photo

One photograph from 1968 — the planet, finally, seen from outside. Students work through how a single image carries science, politics, aesthetics, and an environmental movement at the same time. Proof that objects are never just objects.

Ch. VI
Why the wood block, actually
The work the award was, technically, for.
№ 19 · the actual reason
Classroom Research, Documented
Research Site · AI Detection · Assessment

A small archive of data studies from real classrooms and a real school. Headline reports: a study of 91 papers across AP Language and IB amnesty programs (77% confession rate, <1% false positives, +63% increase in flagging Sep 2025–Jan 2026); an internal Turnitin validation against 44 documents of known authorship; and a plain-language parent summary of the same. The output is a supervised writing protocol for flagged work. The "innovative grading approach" the wood block keeps mentioning lives here.

A daily-tape terminal that scans markets, flags overdone and underdone moves, watches the VIX, and reads the news (yes, including Truth Social) so I don't have to. Powered by Claude Opus 4.7. Pointers, not recommendations. Named after the only Smith allowed to be in two places at once. Definitely not bullet-time.
